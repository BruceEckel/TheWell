I want to create a science fiction novel, and I'd like to know what the best approach is to work with you on planning and structuring it.

The concept is that a group of psychologists are researching human behavior using an advanced experimental machine
that is able to simulate a reality within itself.
They start out by modeling simulated people and quickly discover that their models do not have the richness and depth to produce
useful results.
They then decide that to achieve useful results they need to insert real humans into the simulation, and begin volunteering themselves to go in.
In order that they don't disturb the existing simulation, they must enter as babies with no prior knowledge, only vague intuitions that appear as illogical urges when they make decisions throughout their lives.
The simulation moves at a greater speed than the outside world, so a lifetime in the simulation only takes a few moments.
Initially they only enter for a single lifetime at a time, but realize that this doesn't produce the results they seek.
They begin entering for multiple cycles of lifetimes.
They also discover that the simulated humans that they interact with inside the simulation do not produce realistic results.
To solve this problem they create the simulated humans using "parts" of themselves (as in Internal Family Systems).
The simulated world becomes much richer and more realistic, and because each part driving a simulated human is an unresolved
issue of the researcher, these parts are naturally struggling with each other,
while at the same time drawn to each other within the simulation.
The simulation becomes more compelling to the researchers and they become obsessed with it.
They become lost within the simulation and stuck inside it.

I see different approaches to this:
1. Start within the simulation as fully-believed reality and no awareness of the simulation, discovering clues as to who they really are and eventually escaping.
2. The last researcher realizing that all the others are trapped and entering, trying to arm themselves as best they can, having to enter as an unknowing baby, but somehow carrying the essential information necessary to begin waking the others.
