# Voice and Style (draft 1, 2026-08-22; approved 2026-08-22)

Rules for the manuscript.

Genders assigned (2026-08-22): the protagonist is he/him in every form (Ren, Corin, Dara, Kit); the author identifies with him. Wren is he (a part of Ren; the doubling reads as a near-brother, and comfort is not embodied as a woman). Amara she, Teo he, Sefa she, Noor she, Havel he.

## Point of View and Narrator

- Close third person, past tense, single POV: the current incarnation of Ren throughout.
- The narrator is character-bound: it knows only what the incarnation knows, with one precise exception. At the thresholds (the death-and-birth chapters: "The door," "The choice," "The choice point, awake," and the moment of death itself wherever it falls, as in I.7's final page), the narrator may widen one step beyond the character, which is how the reader perceives the choice point the character cannot. Nowhere else.
- No authorial intrusion, no foreshadowing winks, no future knowledge leaking into narration.

## The Voice Arc

One narrator, three registers, tracking Ren's progress. The prose itself learns to accept comfort:

- **Act I (Corin):** brittle and precise. Short declaratives, clinical diction, sensory detail noticed but held at arm's length. Comfort described from outside, like an anthropologist.
- **Act II (Dara):** wry and tired. The precision remains but admits fatigue and humor. Longer breath in the sentences when Wren is present; the reader should feel the difference before noticing it.
- **Act III (Kit):** the register opens. Sensory warmth admitted: food, weather, touch, liturgy as sound. The same narrator, finally allowed to receive.

## Rendering the Rules of the World

- **The urges:** physical compulsions and aversions, described concretely and never explained. No italics, no signposting. The reader learns to recognize them by recurrence.
- **The contempt:** always armed with good arguments, and the arguments are allowed to be genuinely good on the page. The reader should agree with Corin often.
- **Suffering of background characters:** the standing instruction (no one in the background is at peace) is delivered in single strokes, one detail per person, never dwelt on.
- **Doctrine and scripture:** invented liturgy quoted sparingly and only in fragments. The lodged phrase is fixed at chapter-1 drafting time and never varies by a word thereafter.
- **The subtlety rule** (premise.md): no direct address, no aphorism landings, no closing-line profundity that points at the reader. If a sentence sounds like a thesis, cut it.

## Motif Discipline

Rules: motifs recur without comment; they are never explained; each recurrence is logged below with chapter number so frequency stays low and deliberate.

## Mechanics

- Chapters titled as in the outline. Section breaks within chapters allowed, marked with a centered asterisk line.
- Target 2,500 to 4,000 words per chapter; the novel lands roughly at 90,000 to 110,000 words.
- Sentence style follows the author's standing preferences: no em dashes; prefer shorter sentences; commas or parentheses for asides; comma or semicolon for a pause.
- Dialogue in standard double quotes. No dialect spelling.
- One file per chapter in `manuscript/`, named `act1-ch01-the-funeral.md` and so on.
- Invented proper nouns (the order, the temple, the city, scripture names) get a glossary section in world.md as they are coined, so they stay consistent across chapters.
