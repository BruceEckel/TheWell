# Premise

## Core Concept

A group of psychologists researches human behavior using an advanced experimental machine that can simulate a reality within itself.

## Progression of the Research

1. They start by modeling simulated people. The models lack the richness and depth to produce useful results.
2. To get useful results, they decide to insert real humans into the simulation. They volunteer themselves.
3. To avoid disturbing the existing simulation, they must enter as babies with no prior knowledge. What remains are vague intuitions that surface as illogical urges when they make decisions throughout their lives.
4. The simulation runs faster than the outside world. A lifetime inside takes only a few moments outside.
5. They first enter for a single lifetime at a time. This does not produce the results they seek, so they begin entering for multiple cycles of lifetimes.
6. The simulated humans they interact with inside still do not produce realistic results. To solve this, they create simulated humans from "parts" of themselves, as in Internal Family Systems (IFS).
7. Because each part driving a simulated human is an unresolved issue of a researcher, the parts naturally struggle with each other while also being drawn to each other within the simulation. The simulated world becomes much richer and more realistic.
8. The simulation becomes compelling. The researchers grow obsessed, become lost within it, and are stuck inside.
9. Along the way, researchers attempted to protect themselves against becoming lost using single payload mechanisms: a split-off part alone, trained intuitions alone, or a planted artifact alone. Every single-payload attempt failed, and those researchers became lost within the simulation too.

## Structural Approach (decided 2026-08-22)

The novel uses the rescuer frame containing the discovery-from-inside story. The last researcher realizes all the others are trapped and enters to wake them. They must arm themselves as best they can, yet still enter as an unknowing baby, carrying the essential information necessary to begin waking the others. Because entry erases prior knowledge, the rescuer's story inside the simulation is also a discovery-from-inside story: a fully-believed reality, clues about who they really are, and an eventual escape. The added tension is a mission the protagonist cannot consciously remember.

The two original candidate approaches (pure discovery-from-inside, and the rescuer) were combined rather than chosen between.

## Thematic Questions

- What do we learn about human behavior by living it rather than modeling it?
- Are the parts-driven simulated humans people? What is owed to them?
- Reincarnation and karma reframed as experimental protocol: cycles of lifetimes, carried intuitions, unresolved parts seeking each other.
- Obsession and escapism: the simulation as more compelling than reality.
- Can you heal an unresolved part by meeting it embodied as another person?
- Religion as degraded truth: inside the simulation, the myths are garbled records of real events, and the simulated religions have neutralized every rescue attempt by converting truth into doctrine. The skeptic protagonist's contempt for religion blinds them to accurate data, and that contempt is itself inherited and unexamined, a faith of its own.
