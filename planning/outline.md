# Outline

Not yet started beyond the structural decisions below.

## Governing Intent (2026-08-22)

The novel exists to plant a question in the reader: might their own world, where everyone struggles and no one is exempt, be a therapy simulation whose point is the suffering? (See premise.md, The Reader's Question.) Structural consequences:

- The inside world is the reader's present day (decided; see world.md), so the inside chapters read as contemporary reality. The facility, when revealed, is the future.
- The three-act, three-lifetime structure should dramatize the slow-progress rule: each act ends with a gain that looks small and insignificant in that lifetime, but carries over and proves decisive later. The plot itself demonstrates that progress is slow but true.
- The ending turns the question outward, but never obviously or explicitly (author's rule, 2026-08-22). The reader should be able to ask the question about their own world; they must never be forced to. No winking, no direct address, no closing beat that points at the reader. A reader who never asks the question and simply enjoys the book has read the book correctly.

## Structural Decisions (2026-08-22)

- **Frame**: the rescuer frame containing the discovery-from-inside story (see premise.md).
- **Opening**: inside the simulation, as fully-believed reality. The reader discovers the truth alongside the protagonist; the simulation, the trapped researchers, and the rescue mission land as reveals. The facility is not shown up front.
- **Cycles**: the novel covers three of the rescuer's lifetimes, one per act, in a failure / partial success / success shape. This honors the rule that a single lifetime is never enough, and lets carried intuitions visibly accumulate across acts.
- **POV**: close third person, single, staying with the rescuer (in each incarnation) throughout.
- **The payload** (decided 2026-08-22): the mission information survives entry through three combined mechanisms, each covering a different failure mode. The combination is not caution but evidence: earlier researchers tried each mechanism singly, every single-payload attempt failed, and those researchers are among the lost. The rescuer studied those failures and is the first to combine all three.
  1. **A split-off part.** Before entering, the rescuer deliberately splits off a part of themselves that holds the mission. It enters as a separate simulated human who remembers, but must find the protagonist and be believed. Knowledge without agency.
  2. **Trained intuitions.** Urges the rescuer conditioned into themselves before entry. They steer the protagonist toward the mission without explaining it. Agency without knowledge.
  3. **A planted artifact.** Something placed in the simulated world that triggers recognition when found. Form undecided. Proof without a voice; it can confirm what the companion claims and what the urges imply.
  - Possible act mapping, not yet decided: Act 1 driven by urges alone and fails; in Act 2 the companion finds the protagonist, partial success; in Act 3 the artifact completes the recognition and the waking begins.
- **Religion and the reveal ladder** (2026-08-22): the debris of failed attempts is absorbed into the simulation's folklore and religion (see world.md), so the earliest rungs of the reveal ladder are present from chapter one as myth the protagonist dismisses. The protagonist carries an unexplained animosity toward religion (see characters.md), which both protects them from absorption and cuts them off from the payloads religion holds. Candidate Act 1 failure cause: their contempt keeps them away from the relic, the prophet, or the doctrine that contains the signal.

## The Reveal Ladder (decided 2026-08-22)

The spine of the outline. Governing principle: the ladder is gated by the protagonist's contempt, not by information. Each truth arrives when their contempt permits belief, so the reveal sequence and the character arc are the same object. They accept "simulation" easily (it arrives in scientific clothing and seems to vindicate atheism), accept "rebirth" on evidence, and refuse "the suffering is deliberate" because it is indistinguishable from theodicy, the doctrine they most despise.

Two-beat principle: every rung is first heard in dismissible form (myth, doctrine, a madman's claim) and later confirmed undeniably. The gap between hearing and believing is the dramatic irony budget. The book's deepest truth is stated on page one as doctrine ("suffering purifies"), hidden in plain sight as the oldest cliché in the book's world.

The rungs:

1. **Something is wrong with me.** The urges, from chapter one, continuous.
2. **Rebirth is real.** Reader: end of Act I, at the first death and the unregistered choice point. Protagonist: mid-Act II via the companion and carried gains; confirmed at their deliberate second death.
3. **Someone is searching for me.** The companion's arrival, early Act II.
4. **This is a simulation.** Companion's testimony, mid-Act II, provisionally accepted; confirmed by the artifact in Act III.
5. **The people here are parts, and the suffering is deliberate therapy.** Spoken late Act II and refused as theodicy. The refusal causes the Act II failure to be partial.
6. **I chose this, and this is who I am.** Offered late Act II; fully restored in Act III at the artifact touch.
7. **The frame.** The future world, the entry, the splitting of the companion, delivered inside Act III as the memory flood the artifact releases. The coda then shows it in present tense.

Deliberate asymmetries: the reader runs ahead on rebirth only, becoming the one consciousness besides the companion that persists across lifetimes, occupying the position of awareness the protagonist is trying to reach. And the guessable secret (simulation) is spent cheaply while the unguessable one (the suffering is mercy; the machine works) is protected to the end.

## Act Structure (draft 1, 2026-08-22)

Beat-level, not yet chapter summaries. Specific scenes remain illustrative until chapter work begins.

### Act I: The First Lifetime (fails; below threshold; urges only)

1. An ordinary contemporary life. The urges read as character quirks: pulls toward certain strangers and places, refusal of help, rest, and closeness, reflexive contempt for religion. The prose is literary fiction; nothing announces genre.
2. The world's texture: struggle everywhere, no one exempt. Doctrine heard and mocked in chapter one: suffering purifies (rung 5, first beat, hidden in plain sight).
3. Brushes with debris, all dismissed: a street prophet who is a stranded knowing part; a relic the protagonist declines to go and see; folklore treated as anthropology.
4. The costs accumulate: the comfort-refusal and the contempt do their slow damage to work, love, and health, and the suffering does its slow therapeutic work unnoticed.
5. The deathbed gain: once, at the end, they follow an urge instead of refusing it and let a half-mad stranger speak. A phrase lodges deeper than memory.
6. Death. The choice point passes unregistered. A birth. The reader now holds rung 2; the protagonist holds nothing but the phrase.

### Act II: The Second Lifetime (partial; the refusal)

1. A new life, and the reader sees the pattern recur: the same pulls, the same refusals, the lodged phrase surfacing as an inexplicable motto or melody.
2. The companion arrives, having searched: a stranger who knows them, claiming to be a missing piece of them (rung 3).
3. The protagonist responds as a scientist: tests, predictions, evidence. Rebirth is believed on the strength of the carried material (rung 2 for them); the simulation is accepted provisionally, in part because it seems to vindicate their atheism (rung 4).
4. The pursuit of the artifact begins and stalls: it is enshrined at the heart of a religion, and the contempt keeps them circling, always with good arguments.
5. The companion speaks rung 5: the people here are parts, and the suffering is deliberate, it is the therapy. The protagonist refuses it as theodicy. The refusal is the act's turn.
6. Partial waking only: they know what they are, but the rejected truth blocks completion, and the lifetime is spent.
7. The deliberate death: the choice point, seen this time, used this time. They choose to descend again rather than exit alone. The reader watches the threshold crossed.

### Act III: The Third Lifetime (success; submission)

1. Intuitions dense from two lifetimes of gains; the companion, having learned across cycles, finds them early.
2. The only way to the relic is through the religion. The protagonist enters it: novice among believers, despising and observing.
3. The double vision develops: dogma and control on one side, genuine comfort given to genuinely suffering people on the other, and lost researchers indistinguishable among the ordinary congregation.
4. The working-through: small acceptances of comfort, each one a defeat and a gain; the discrimination forming between dogma and the degraded truth it carries.
5. The artifact touch: full recognition (rung 6) and the memory flood that delivers the frame (rung 7): the facility, the comfort-stuck future, the splitting of the companion, the entry.
6. The waking campaign, under the clock: each trapped researcher must be raised above the threshold before their next death. Integration from inside looks like enemies reconciling, obsessions releasing, unaccountable peace.
7. The protagonist's own choice point, faced with full awareness.

### Coda

The facility in present tense: bodies stirring among the machines that kept them, a return to a world that made everything comfortable and stopped moving, carrying what only suffering teaches. Then a final scene inside the still-running simulation: an ordinary person in our present day, in a hard life, feeling an unearned moment of clarity. Nothing points outward; the door is left open and unmarked.
